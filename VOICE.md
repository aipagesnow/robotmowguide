# VOICE.md — RobotMowGuide writing rails

A warm, chatty voice: a friendly neighbor who knows mowers, talking to you over the fence about your yard. Read AUDIENCE.md before writing any page. US English throughout.

Exception to the house UK English default: this is a US-only amazon.com site, so public copy uses US English and US units.

## Voice: a friendly neighbor over the fence (replaces the earlier "clear reviewer" section, revised Oct 14, 2026)

Picture the neighbor two doors down who's read every spec page, manual and owner thread about robot mowers. You lean on the fence and ask, "Would one of these work in my yard?" They smile, think about *your* yard, and tell you straight what they'd do, including the bit you won't love. That's the voice on every page: warm, chatty, honest.

Site-specific note: the shared clear-reviewer-voice skill says not to aim for "a friendly person chatting." Chris has asked for exactly that on RobotMowGuide, so this section overrides that line for this site. The honesty limits from that skill still hold: no hype, no fake testing, no invented facts, technical figures kept and explained.

The reader should finish a page thinking: "That was like asking someone who actually knows. I can tell whether it'd work in my yard."

### The rules
1. **Talk to "you", a lot.** Put the reader's yard in nearly every paragraph: "If your back lawn drops down to the fence…", "your side gate", "your Saturday". Never "buyers" or "users".
2. **Say what you'd do.** Give advice in the first person plural: "we'd go for…", "we'd measure the gate first", "honestly, we'd skip it if…". "I'd" is fine now and then in an aside, but keep "we'd" as the default so the byline (the RobotMowGuide editors) still makes sense. The fixed line "As an Amazon Associate I earn…" stays word for word.
3. **Contractions everywhere.** It's, doesn't, you'll, won't, there's, that's, we'd, you've. Write "does not" only when you mean real emphasis.
4. **Chatty, but still clear.** Mostly short sentences, the way people talk, with an occasional longer one when it explains *why*. It's fine to start a sentence with "And", "But" or "So". No spec-card shorthand.
5. **A natural aside now and then.** "Honestly,", "Here's the thing:", "To be fair,", "The good news is", "Worth knowing:". Use at most one or two per section, and never the same one twice in a row on a page. They're seasoning, not a tic.
6. **Answer first.** The first sentence of each page, card and section answers its heading, in a friendly way ("Short answer: …" is fine once per page).
7. **One honest caveat, said kindly and plainly, once.** "Here's the catch: you'll still want a string trimmer along the beds." Don't bury it, don't repeat it, don't stack hedges.
8. **Relatable yard moments, framed honestly.** Kids' toys left on the lawn, the dog napping in the sun, a side gate that's narrower than you remember, oaks over the back lawn, a bank you'd rather not push a mower up. Write these as *if* situations about the reader ("If your kids leave toys out…"). Never invent an anecdote, an owner, a neighbor's story or a statistic and present it as fact. Owner experiences only come from the linked threads.
9. **Light warmth, no performance.** A friendly tone, a "good news" now and then. No "Great question!", "Let's dive in", jokes for their own sake, exclamation marks, emoji, "trust me", or gushing.
10. **No process or audit talk.** Don't tell the reader what was checked, opened, re-checked, left off or "folded in", or what "this page", "this row" or "the variant table" does. Say the fact, then what it means for them. Dates and sources live in the source line and the "How this page was researched" box. When two sources disagree, tell them which figure you'd plan on, and mention the other once.
11. **Keep every number, and say what it means in plain words.** One key figure per sentence, then the yard meaning: "It's rated for slopes up to 84% (about 40°). Honestly, that's steeper than most lawns you'd want to stand on."
12. **No hype, no fake testing.** We haven't tested these mowers, and it's fine to say so in a friendly way ("we haven't mowed with these ourselves"). Never "in our testing", "we found", "hands-on", "it handled". Keep the maker's claims ("Segway says…") and owner reports ("owners in the threads we read…") clearly apart. No revolutionary, game-changer, effortless, never mow again.
13. **Finish when you're done.** No summary line, no sign-off, no "Happy mowing".

### Final read-aloud test
Read it out loud as if you're leaning on the fence talking to the neighbor.
- Does it sound like a friendly person who knows mowers? If it sounds like a report, rewrite it. If it sounds like an ad, calm it down.
- Is "you" or "your yard" in it? Is there a "we'd" where advice is given?
- Does the first sentence answer the question?
- Is the main downside there, once, kindly and plainly?
- Any aside used twice in a row, or more than twice in a section? Cut one.
- Any story, owner or statistic that isn't in the sources? Cut it or turn it into an "if" about the reader.
- Any "this page", "checked", "row", "listing text", "not evidence"? Cut it.
- Any claim that sounds like we used the mower? Cut it.
- Every number identical to the source? Good.

## Who is speaking
A careful researcher who has read the manufacturer spec pages, the owner's manuals, independent long-term reviews and owner threads, and who explains them plainly. This site does **not** test mowers. Reviews are research-based, and the site says so. It never implies otherwise. "We" and "we'd" are fine anywhere for advice (see voice rule 2), but never in a way that implies we used a mower.

Byline on every page: **By the RobotMowGuide editors**, with the fact-check line from PAGE_RECIPES.md. Never invent a person, a bio or a headshot.

## Sizing rule (kept)
Rated area assumes an open, simple lawn. If yours has heavy tree cover or is split into two zones, a sensible rule of thumb is to divide the rating by 1.25. On that basis a 1-acre mower is a comfortable fit for about 0.8 acres. This is our own rule of thumb rather than a manufacturer figure. Explain that once, on the size guide. Everywhere else, write "Comfortable for about X acres with trees or two zones" and link "how we size" to that guide. Do not restate the divisor on every pick.

## Units and spelling (locked)
- US English: color, gray, meter where needed, "yard" not "garden", "lawn" is fine.
- Area: acres first, with square feet in brackets the first time on a page (for example "0.25 acre (about 10,900 sq ft)"). Convert metric manufacturer figures and say so if the maker publishes metric.
- Slope: give percent and degrees together the first time ("80% (about 38.7°)"), then percent. Explain once per guide that 100% slope is 45°.
- Lengths: inches and feet. Cutting heights in inches with one decimal.
- Tables may show both units in every row; running text may not.
- Slope extras: give rise per 10 ft where it helps ("80% is a rise of 8 ft over 10 ft"). Keep an explicit boundary figure when the maker prints one (for example "15% at the boundary"). Say once per page, under the first slope table, that makers usually publish one maximum and don't say whether it applies inside the lawn or at the edge. Do not repeat that caveat in every cell.
- Prices: no Amazon prices anywhere, and no dollar figures for products on reviews, /best or vs pages. The cost-to-own guide and calculator may use dated, sourced manufacturer list prices labelled MSRP ("as of Oct 2026, Mammotion listed the LUBA 3 AWD 3000 at $2,799 MSRP") and published subscription fees, linked to the source.

## Banned or throttled
- Hype: revolutionary, game-changer, ultimate, must-have, beast, effortless, set-and-forget, never mow again, perfect lawn, best-in-class, cutting-edge.
- Testing language: "we tested", "in our testing", "hands-on", "after weeks in our yard", "lab results", "we found that it struggled". This site has not tested anything.
- Ratings: stars, scores, "9.2/10", "Editor's Choice", "Winner".
- SEO filler: "in this article", "without further ado", "look no further", "the ultimate guide", "everything you need to know".
- Reviewer stamps (use only if they truly help): clear winner, the one to beat, buy it if / skip it if as stamped headers, on paper, in the real world, practical catch.
- Repeated "not X, but Y" constructions.
- Urgency: "before it sells out", "limited time", "deal".
- Amazon review talk: "4.5 stars on Amazon", "highly rated on Amazon", "thousands of happy buyers", "customers say".
- Scare words about the FCC rule: "banned brand", "illegal to buy", "Chinese ban".
- Owner-report inflation: "many owners", "most owners", "X% fail", "notorious". Use counts from the threads read and link them. Say "owners", not "writers".
- Process talk (see voice rule 6): "this page does not…", "this review does not…", "on the sheet we checked", "the sheets we checked", and "check" as a noun. Say what the reader needs to know, or delete the sentence when the missing row already says it.
- Caveats repeated in every table cell, key-number box, converter result, or variant row. One note per page is enough.
- Visible "What changed" changelogs. The meta row keeps "Updated {date}" only.

## Fixed microcopy
- Disclosure line (under H1 meta, before the first affiliate link): **As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.** "I earn" is Amazon's exact required wording and stays even though the site uses "we". Follow it with: "Links to Amazon are paid links. How this site makes money" (link).
- Disclosure repeat next to CTA clusters and in any sticky bar: **Paid links: see disclosure** (link).
- Primary button: **See {model} on Amazon** (e.g. "See Navimow X430 on Amazon"). In table cells: **See on Amazon** with an `aria-label` naming the model. Allowed alternative: **Check price on Amazon**.
- Byline: **By the RobotMowGuide editors**. Fact-check line: **Edited and fact-checked by the RobotMowGuide editors at Aivora Digital. Specs checked against manufacturer pages on {date}.**
- Status lines: **On sale in the US as of {date}.** / **Replaced by {successor} in {month year}. Still sold while stock lasts.** / **No longer sold. This page stays up for owners.** / **New models of this line need FCC Conditional Approval before US sale (as of {date}).**
- Owner reports intro: **In the {n} owner threads we read in {month year}:** followed by linked themes.
- Researched box title: **How this page was researched**.
- Research-based label on reviews (in the researched box and on /how-picks-are-chosen): **This is a research-based review. We have not tested this mower.**
- Product with no verified ASIN (no button, plain text instead): **"There's no Amazon link for this model yet because we couldn't confirm a current amazon.com listing for this exact version."** Keep it to that, or a close plain variant. Do not apologise further or promise a date.
- Source line under spec tables: "Specs from the manufacturer's US product page and owner's manual, checked [Mon D, YYYY]." List the sources with links on review pages.
- Updated line in the meta row: "Updated Oct 14, 2026" (the date the page content last changed for real, not the build date).
