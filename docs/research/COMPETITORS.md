## Patterns
1. **Big publishers test, but each tests one yard.** Wirecutter has PA cool-season grass, the Verge has SC centipede and sand, Bob Vila has FL St. Augustine. Nobody maps picks to *yard type × grass × slope × tree cover* systematically.
2. **Niche sites are databases or syntheses with no first-hand evidence.** Some say so honestly (smartlawnbots, mowscout). Some overclaim (mowerbotlab). Google's reviews guidance rewards first-hand evidence. Overclaiming testing is a trust and FTC risk we must not copy.
3. **FCC coverage is thin or wrong everywhere.** Wirecutter's date is off by a day and it misses the waiver and Conditional Approval. Mowerbotlab has the wrong percentage and is stale. Bob Vila says nothing. CR has one line. **Nobody has a maintained, primary-sourced tracker.**
4. **Subscription and running-cost data is scattered.** Wirecutter mentions MOVA GPS at $50/yr after year 1 and the Worx tracker at $100 one-off. The Verge says "NetRTK free for life". Mowscout has a checklist. **Nobody has a complete per-model table** of 4G/NetRTK/tracking costs and what happens after the free period.
5. **Warm-season grass** (St. Augustine, Bermuda, Zoysia, centipede) gets one-line mentions only. Brand caveats like eufy's "not suitable for St. Augustine" aren't surfaced.
6. **Model churn.** CR still rates superseded models. Competitor lists mix legacy (Dreame A1/A2, LUBA 2) and current models.

## Trust posture recommendation
- Until we own units, label reviews **"research-based review"**. Say that up front in the methodology (as smartlawnbots does). Never write "we tested".
- When we do test, show our own photos and video, the yard specs, and dates.
- Put a "last verified" date and source links on every spec. Changelog the FCC page.
- Real author with a bio. FTC disclosure near the affiliate links.
