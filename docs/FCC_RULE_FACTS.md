# FCC rule on foreign-produced robots (Jul 28, 2026): facts for the explainer

Research notes for the tracker page `/guides/fcc-robot-mower-ban` (built in round 2, kept updated). Facts checked Oct 10, 2026; the research pack's full notes are in `docs/research/FCC-RULE.md`. Re-check every source the day the page is built and again at each refresh. The page must be neutral and factual: what changed, what didn't, what it means for a buyer. No politics, no speculation about trade talks.

## What happened
- **Jul 27, 2026:** a White House-convened Executive Branch interagency body sent the FCC two National Security Determinations, one covering foreign-produced "advanced robotic devices" and one covering connected power inverters. [S2, S3]
- **Jul 28, 2026:** the FCC's Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau added to the FCC Covered List: "Foreign-produced advanced robotic devices, except advanced robotic devices which have been granted a Conditional Approval by DoW" (Department of War). Public notice DA 26-786. [S2, S3]
- Equipment on the Covered List cannot receive FCC equipment authorization, including through the Supplier's Declaration of Conformity route. Most electronic devices need FCC authorization before they can be imported, marketed or sold in the US. [S1]

## Does it cover robot lawn mowers?
- The FCC notice does not name lawn mowers. Its definition of "advanced robotic device" is a mechanical mobile device that moves on the ground, operates at a distance from a human based on commands or sensor data, weighs over 4.4 lb with its dock, and contains a sensor, a network connection of at least 200 kbps (including Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular) and navigation/control software. [S1]
- Connected wire-free robot mowers fit that description. The FCC confirmed to PCMag that the rule "covers both robot vacuums and robot lawn mowers" (PCMag, Jul 29, 2026; secondary). [S7] Trade coverage treats them as covered too. [S4, S5] The FCC FAQ also confirms that pool-cleaning robots that operate above water are not excluded, which shows how broadly the definition is read. [S1]
- Write it as: "The notice doesn't name robot mowers, but its definition covers connected ones, and the FCC told PCMag the rule covers robot lawn mowers."
- Our interpretation, labelled as such: a cheap boundary-wire mower with **no** app or radio may fall outside the definition (it needs ≥200 kbps connectivity). Any wire mower with a Bluetooth or Wi-Fi app very likely qualifies.

## What did not change
- Models that already received FCC authorization can still be imported, marketed and sold. [S1, S2]
- People who already own one can keep using it. [S1]
- Devices authorized before Jul 28, 2026 "may continue to receive software and firmware updates that mitigate harm to U.S. consumers at least until January 1, 2029", including security patches and OS-compatibility updates (OET public notice DA 26-789). [S8] After that date it depends on further FCC action.
- Repairs, and replacement with identical parts, are not prohibited. [S1]

## Who counts as "foreign-produced"
- Anything that is not a "domestic end product" under 48 CFR § 25.101(a): it must be manufactured in the US **and** domestic components must exceed 65% of component cost for items delivered 2024–2028 (75% from 2029). [S1]
- The rule is country-neutral; the maker's nationality does not matter, only where and how the device is produced. [S1] So a US or European brand with mowers made abroad is covered the same way.

## Conditional Approval route
- The Department of War can grant Conditional Approval to a specific device or class of devices; approved devices are listed on the FCC website. Applications go to conditional-approvals@fcc.gov. [S1, S2]
- **Sep 9, 2026:** the FCC's Conditional Approvals table lists Husqvarna AB 305v IQ, 310v IQ, 420v IQ and 440v IQ robotic platforms (order DA 26-957; no end date listed). Husqvarna expects US launches in 2027 and says already-approved models on the market are unaffected. [S9, S6]
- Other robotics approvals so far are not mowers: ANSCER Robotics industrial AMRs (Sep 18, DA 26-996) and a Matic Robots floor cleaner (Oct 2, DA 26-1063). [S9]
- At the Oct 10, 2026 check (table "Updated October 9, 2026"), **Husqvarna is the only robot-mower brand with a Conditional Approval.** Navimow, Mammotion, Dreame, ECOVACS, MOVA, eufy, Sunseeker, Worx, Greenworks and others have none; their existing authorized models remain legal to sell. [S9]
- Approval requires disclosing ownership, a full bill of materials with countries of origin, and a time-bound US onshoring plan with quarterly updates (Skadden summary; secondary). [S10]
- PCMag reports an application deadline of Jan 1, 2028 (secondary, **unconfirmed** against the FCC guidance; don't state it as fact until confirmed). [S7]

## Brand statements (quote only with the original)
- Segway Navimow: "no immediate impact … all Navimow models currently sold in the U.S. have already received FCC equipment authorization"; it is "evaluating compliance pathways for future products" (quoted by SpecsLib; original not located). [S4]
- Mammotion: no public comment found (Reddit thread, Oct 2026).

## Pending risk to watch
- On Aug 10, 2026 the FCC proposed banning continued importation and marketing of some **previously authorized** covered equipment. The named targets were drones (NDAA §1709 equipment, Anzu Robotics), not robot mowers. The mechanism (47 CFR 2.939(e)) could in principle be used for robots later. No such proposal exists for robot mowers as of Oct 10, 2026. State it calmly in the "open questions" section. [S11]

## What it means for a buyer (judgement, label it as such)
- The robot mowers on sale in the US today can still be bought, and existing owners keep getting basic updates.
- New models from foreign factories (for example, several edge-trimming mowers shown at IFA 2026) may not reach the US until they get Conditional Approval or are produced domestically. Manufacturers at IFA did not commit to US dates. [S5]
- In practice, the US lineup may change more slowly than Europe's for a while, and some current models could sell out without a direct replacement. That is a reason to buy on fit for your yard today, not to wait for an announced successor.
- How to check a model: look up its FCC ID in the FCC Equipment Authorization search (https://apps.fcc.gov/oetcf/eas/reports/GenericSearch.cfm). A device with a valid FCC ID or SDoC compliance information is authorized. [S1] Example IDs seen on third-party mirrors (verify in the FCC database before quoting): Navimow X3 series 2BAXN-MR0005; Mammotion 2BVPU-LUBAMN2; a LUBA 3 grant 2BFWS-LUBA3SH.
- Grey imports and EU-only models can't lawfully be operated in the US without FCC authorization. Don't link to them.
- Long-term support is a real buying factor: every review carries a "support outlook" line.

## Common errors on competing pages (don't repeat them)
- The PSHSB notice is dated **Jul 28**, not Jul 29.
- The domestic-content threshold rises to **75%** from 2029, not 70%.
- Many pages miss the Jan 1, 2029 update waiver and Husqvarna's Conditional Approval.

## Words to avoid
"banned brand", "illegal to buy", "Chinese ban". The rule is country-neutral and does not stop existing models being sold.
